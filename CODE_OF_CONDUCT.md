# Code of conduct

## The short version

Be decent. Assume the other person is trying to help. Criticise the code, not
the person who wrote it.

## What we expect

Rhizomium is a tool for artists, and the people who show up here range from
career graphics programmers to someone who has never opened a pull request
before. Both are welcome and both are useful. Specifically:

- **Be patient with beginners.** A question that seems obvious to you was not
  obvious to you once either.
- **Review code, not people.** "This recompiles the shader on every drag" is
  useful. "You clearly did not read the docs" is not, and says the same thing
  worse.
- **Disagree honestly and drop it when it is settled.** Strong technical
  disagreement is fine and often the point. Relitigating a closed decision in
  every thread is not.
- **Take a no gracefully.** Not every patch gets merged. A maintainer declining
  your change is not a judgement of you.
- **Respect the scope of someone's time.** This is largely volunteer work.
  Nobody is obliged to answer immediately, or at all.

## What is not acceptable

- Harassment of any kind, in public or private.
- Insults, demeaning comments, or personal attacks.
- Discrimination or derogatory remarks based on gender, gender identity or
  expression, sexual orientation, disability, neurotype, appearance, body size,
  race, ethnicity, nationality, age, religion, or experience level.
- Sexual language or imagery, or unwanted sexual attention.
- Publishing anyone's private information without their explicit permission.
- Sustained disruption: derailing threads, bad-faith argument, or refusing to
  let a settled decision rest.

## Scope

This applies in every project space — issues, pull requests, discussions,
commit messages, the docs — and to anyone representing the project in public.
It also applies to behaviour outside these spaces where that behaviour makes
someone unsafe participating here.

## Reporting

Email **conduct@tenderworld.org** — that address is not routed yet, so until
it is, write to **me@ashkankhodaverdi.com** instead.

Reports are read by the project maintainers and kept confidential. You will get
an acknowledgement, and you will be told what came of it.

If your report concerns a maintainer, say so — it will be handled by someone
else, and if that is not possible we will tell you rather than quietly handle
it ourselves.

You will not be penalised for reporting in good faith, and you do not have to
be the target of the behaviour to report it.

## Enforcement

Depending on severity and pattern, a response may be:

1. **A private word** — an explanation of what was wrong and why.
2. **A warning** — with a stated consequence if it continues.
3. **A temporary ban** from project spaces.
4. **A permanent ban.**

Maintainers may remove or edit comments, commits, issues and pull requests that
breach this document, and will say when they have done so.

Enforcement decisions are made by people, in context, and they are not a
lookup table. We will apply them as consistently as we can and explain our
reasoning when asked.
